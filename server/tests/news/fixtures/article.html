River town votes for a tidal barrier

By Staff Reporter

Councillors in the river town of Wexley voted on Tuesday night to build a tidal barrier across the estuary, ending a debate that has divided the town for almost ten years. The vote passed by seven votes to four after a meeting that ran past midnight, with more than two hundred residents crowding into the town hall to watch.

The barrier, a row of steel gates that would close during storm surges, is expected to cost about forty million pounds. Supporters say it will protect around three thousand homes that flooded twice in the past decade, and that insurers have already begun refusing cover to some streets near the quay.

Opponents, including the local fishing cooperative, argue that the gates will change how silt moves through the estuary and could ruin the oyster beds that have been worked by the same families for generations. They asked the council to wait for a second environmental study, which is due next autumn.

The council's leader said the town could not afford another winter of uncertainty. Construction is due to begin in the spring if the regional government approves the funding, and the gates would take about three years to build. A public exhibition of the final design will open at the library next month.

The fishing cooperative said it would consider a legal challenge, and called on residents to attend the exhibition and ask questions about how the oyster beds will be monitored once the work begins.
